## Synthesis

Read together, the fifteen papers separate into four groups, and the boundary between them is where
this project sits.

**Configuration security is measured, not reasoned about.** Verdet et al., Hu et al. and Vo et al.
all analyse Terraform through tools that evaluate one resource at a time. Hu et al. report that
resources with dependencies attract more alerts — the relational signal is visible in their data —
yet the analysis stays per-manifest. Detection quality improves (Wen & Ping reach 97% on real
misconfigurations) without the finding ever acquiring a justification an auditor could accept.

**Graphs are used reactively, on estates that already exist.** Elmiger et al. and Palma et al. both
demonstrate that graph structure exposes attack paths a per-entity review misses, which is the same
premise this project starts from. Both operate after deployment. Neither reasons at design time,
and neither binds a path to a regulatory clause.

**Compliance knowledge and configuration knowledge live in separate graphs.** Jovanovski et al.
model relationships between standard clauses; Elmiger et al. model relationships between cloud
entities. No paper in this set connects the two. That missing crosswalk is why a compliance system
can retrieve the clause but cannot say which resource property satisfies it.

**Intent is an input, never a recovered quantity.** Nekrasov et al. state the problem most directly:
knowledge injection lifts technical correctness sharply while intent alignment plateaus. Review
tools assume no intent at all and check against fixed rules. Recovering the intent an existing
estate was built to serve — and reporting how confident that recovery is — appears nowhere.

Some entries are adjacent rather than upstream, and should be presented that way: Jia et al. reason
over threat-intelligence prose, Guo et al. survey graph counterfactuals with no security domain,
Morello et al. verify a compliance claim that someone else derived. They belong in the survey
because they establish the techniques, not because they leave a hole shaped like this project.
